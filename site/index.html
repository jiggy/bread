A little deeper

The details that matter to you

The short answers are here. The Bread License contains the exact terms.

Can I use it for free?

Personal projects are always free. Work for an employer or client follows the business-use rules.

Evaluation is free at any company. Take as long as you need to decide whether the software fits. Once you rely on it for everyday work, business-use rules apply—even on your laptop. Building a product before using the software in regular business operations is also free.

Small companies can use it for free at work. Count revenue and qualifying funding together across the parent company and the companies it controls, usually over the last twelve months. At US$1m or more, you need paid or existing coverage.

Investment and business grants count; ordinary loans, valuations, and promised funding do not. If your company grows past the free allowance in good faith, you get 90 days to arrange coverage or stop using it for uncovered business work. See exactly what counts and when to check.

What happens when I cancel or stop renewing?

You can keep using the software your purchase covers. A company license includes existing versions and updates published during the period you paid for, apart from any exclusions shown before purchase. Stop renewing and you keep those versions; later updates need their own coverage.

Bought an app with the license included? You can keep using, customizing, and repairing it, including using its runtime directly within the covered app. A separate standalone deployment needs its own permission.

Hosting lasts for the period you paid for. It does not include a right to export the software and run it yourself unless that permission is part of your purchase or you have a separate license.

Are my customers covered when I redistribute or host Bread-licensed software?

You can include a license for that software with your paid product or service. Sign up as a publisher for that offering and pay 1% of its covered receipts. Your customers then need no separate license for the included software within that product, whatever their size. Or distribute it with clear notice that customers need their own permission. Competing products and hosts get the same terms.

The 1% rate stays fixed while your product’s agreement continues. Reporting and payment are annual by default.

Bread applies to the included software. Your independent application code and unrelated products keep their own licenses.

Free plans follow the usual free-use rules. If you give customers software copies or environments to run their own workflows, each customer needs free eligibility or an existing license. Your paid plan does not cover free users; you also need permission to operate the free plan.

Does your app just deliver results? Customers need no separate license merely because your backend uses Bread-licensed software. You need permission to run it, but you can use an ordinary company license or free eligibility. Hosting customers’ own programs or workflows needs customer coverage; letting them configure your app’s existing features does not.

Each separately licensed component has its own obligations. One Bread purchase does not cover them all. The distribution guide explains combining dependencies, reselling products, and telling customers what is included.

When I use Bread-licensed software, what source code must I share?

Share the Bread-licensed code you distribute, including your changes. Give recipients source code, build instructions, and license notices they can keep. Separate files containing only your own application code can stay private. Using or hosting the software privately does not, by itself, require publishing changes.

Sharing a modified version does not add another license fee. People using your modified version follow the original project’s license and payment rules. You cannot charge a second permission fee for your changes, but you can charge for copies, support, and independently licensed additions. New versions from the original project still need the right coverage.

About Bread

Why Bread?

Because making useful things should put bread on the table. Bread connects accessible software with a livelihood for the people who make it.

Is Bread open source?

No. Software under Bread is source-available: you can read and modify its code, but some business uses require payment. Those restrictions do not meet the Open Source Definition.

Bread does not become open source automatically after a waiting period.

Can I use Bread for my own software?

Yes—adopting Bread is free, with no membership or approval required. Choose your prices and how long each purchase includes new updates. Keep Bread’s free allowances, customer rights, and standard 1% redistribution terms intact.

Name the software and who licenses it. Publish your prices and give companies and redistributors a way to sign up. The adoption guide explains the setup and records you need.
