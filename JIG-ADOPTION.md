## What this would mean for Jig users

Personal projects, eligible companies, and genuine evaluation follow sections
2–3 without registration. A company purchase covers its named company group's
ordinary operations. Canceling sponsorship leaves the purchased update period
intact. Updates published after it ends are not covered by that purchase;
permission for already-covered software remains. Sponsor benefits end according
to their purchased terms. Jig's proposed annual prices are $10, $100, $1,000, and $10,000
under schedule P-0.5.

Redistributors can include Jig licenses in a paid product through section 6's
1% agreement. Customers can run and modify the included Jig runtime, including
adding Flows, whatever their company's size. That license covers the product,
not unrelated deployments or their entire company. Recipients of free Jig copies
or free customer environments need their own free eligibility or other permission.
People who only receive an application's results need no customer Jig license.

### A $100 assistant with Jig included

An enrolled publisher sells a paid assistant containing an identified Jig version
to a large company. Assume $100 received, with no tax, refund, or other deduction.
The publisher owes $1. The company's rights to its delivered copy continue even
if the publisher closes.

Independent application source can stay private. Distributed Jig source and
modifications must be supplied. The company cannot extract Jig to license an
unrelated deployment under that purchase.

### Contributions remain a separate choice

Jig's proposed [contributor agreement](CONTRIBUTOR-AGREEMENT.md) gives broad
permission for future licensing choices if properly accepted. Bread does not
require that agreement. It grants no rights over unsubmitted modifications or
separately licensed work.
