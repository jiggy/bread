Bread

Software licensing grounded in reciprocity.

Free to build, learn, and grow under $1M. Dependable, perpetual rights when you scale. A straightforward 1% distribution royalty. No runtime telemetry or artificial feature-gating.

The Bargain

Useful work, meaningful access, and clear commitments on both sides. Creators earn a livelihood from their software; builders get rights they can depend on.

What you want to do The terms
Use it personally Free forever. No account, no credit card, no debt of gratitude.
Run in an organization under $1M Free operational use for businesses and groups with under $1M annual financial scale (revenue + qualifying funding).
Evaluate at any scale Free evaluation for teams of any size. No expiration countdown, no mandatory sales calls or registration.
Run in an organization over $1M Pay the published, self-serve band. An annual purchase provides permanent rights to all covered releases. Non-renewal never revokes acquired software versions.
Embed in a commercial product or SaaS Pay 1% of product receipts under a standard publisher agreement. Your customers get included coverage automatically. No enterprise negotiation, no per-seat tax.
Modify, redistribute, or fork Inspect, customize, and redistribute the source. Upstream coverage includes community modifications without stacked license fees. Private backend changes stay private.
Deliver hosted outputs or workflows Merely receiving answers or outputs from software requires no license. Outputs belong entirely to their creator.

Principles Behind the Bread

Room to Begin

People must be able to experiment and build before they have the resources to fund their tools. Generous free allowances make access genuine and complete.

Sustainable Livelihoods

Maintainers shouldn't need consulting, cloud-hosting side ventures, or VC funding just to survive. Earning from the software itself is legitimate and dignified.

Promises You Can Plan Around

Acquired rights are perpetual for covered releases. If you stop renewing, you keep what you paid for. No sudden rug-pulls, lock-in, or retroactive fee increases.

Respect the Next Builder

Downstream developers get a clear 1% revenue path to ship products with embedded software. Competition is welcomed; forks are protected.

One Complete Product

No crippled "community editions" vs "enterprise binaries". No license key activation servers, runtime phone-home telemetry, or kill switches.

Human Dignity

No groveling for tips, guilt-tripping users, or predatory traps. Free users are welcomed as equals. Commercial compliance is simple, self-serve, and transparent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is it called Bread?
Bread is a staple. It feeds people, symbolizes daily sustenance, and stands for honest work and tangible value. It reflects our conviction that creators deserve to earn their daily bread, and builders deserve wholesome, reliable ingredients.
Is Bread "Open Source"?
No, and it does not pretend to be. The Bread License is source-available. Open Source (under the OSI definition) prohibits discrimination against fields of endeavor and commercial use, which prevents maintainers from asking commercial beneficiaries to pay. Bread makes source code inspectable and modifiable while conditioning scaled commercial use on fair reciprocity.
How does Bread compare to FSL (Functional Source License) or BSL?
FSL and BSL rely on a time delay: code is non-commercial for 2 to 4 years, then converts to Apache 2.0 or MIT.

Bread takes an economic scale & reciprocity approach:
  • No waiting 2 years: Small businesses, startups under $1M, students, researchers, and individuals get full access immediately, forever.
  • Sustainable ongoing revenue: Thriving organizations pay transparent fees that permanently sustain the project.
  • Built-in 1% distribution: Instead of forbidding SaaS or competing products, Bread explicitly gives builders a clear, affordable 1% revenue share model to redistribute or host the software.
What about AGPLv3?
AGPLv3 attempts to prevent free-riding through copyleft network disclosure. In practice, enterprise legal teams often ban AGPL entirely out of fear of accidental proprietary code leakage. Furthermore, AGPL doesn't directly provide maintainers with revenue. Bread gives enterprises legal clarity: private application logic and separate files stay private, while commercial scale simply pays a fair, published fee.
What about "Open Core"?
Open Core creates a split personality: maintainers withhold the best features (SSO, audit logs, clustering, high availability) behind closed proprietary licenses. Bread offers one complete product. Every user runs the exact same code with all features enabled.
How does the 1% commercial distribution work?
If you embed Bread software in a paid SaaS or commercial application, you don't have to beg for a custom OEM contract or pay six-figure upfront license fees. You simply enroll, pay 1% of covered receipts, and your customers receive full coverage automatically.
What happens if a company stops renewing its subscription?
They keep perpetual rights to every release published up to the end of their update term. They simply stop receiving coverage for new releases published in the future. Acquired rights cannot be retroactively revoked.
Are there seat counts, license keys, or network phone-home checks?
Never. Bread strictly prohibits compulsory telemetry, license servers, or activation keys. Compliance is based on self-declaration and published accounting thresholds.
Can I use Bread for my own software project?
Yes! Bread is an independent, freely reusable standard. Read ADOPTION.md to see how to fill in the adoption notice and publish your direct pricing schedule.
