# Jig licensing proposal

**Discussion candidate only. No live license, price, seller appointment, privacy program, or checkout is changed.** The user authorized drafting and independent adversarial review, not publication of operative terms.

Read [LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md) for the single candidate license, [PRICING.md](PRICING.md) for the customer explanation and proposed fee schedule, and [CONTRIBUTOR-AGREEMENT.md](CONTRIBUTOR-AGREEMENT.md) for incoming rights. The earlier v0.1/v0.2 packs are historical background, not competing current proposals.

## Frozen policy for this review

- One complete source-available Jig product; FLOW and Sley stay separate.
- Free personal use and ordinary use below $1m annual revenue plus qualifying funding. No employee count. Automatic purpose-limited evaluation, no registration or fixed expiry.
- Four direct price bands: $10 at $1m, $100 at $10m, $1,000 at $100m, $10,000 at $1bn. Prices buy update coverage; covered release rights are perpetual, including paid sponsor months. Prospective price changes only.
- Product-scoped paid distribution/hosting coverage at 1% receipts, not company-wide rights. Source for distributed Jig modifications, not independent app code. No broad competing-use restriction.
- Free products follow ordinary eligibility. The user expressly rejected a minimum annual fee or other blanket-coverage route for zero-revenue products.
- Founder remains steward; future contributors grant broad prospective relicensing powers. Existing acquired rights and third-party terms survive.
- No runtime DRM, compulsory telemetry, customer financial questionnaires for publishers, or per-Flow fees.

## Implemented drafting choices

Free freemium plans use ordinary eligibility. Acquired delivered-copy rights and repairs survive; otherwise-uncovered official releases require a new applicable entitlement. Paid access can include product permission; a wholly free offering does not acquire universal permission from a token paid plan elsewhere. A real low-price product can supply scoped coverage to a large company: this is an accepted tradeoff. Same-Group transactions cannot turn internal adoption into unrelated-customer coverage.

Reporting is quarterly with small balances settled annually; the no-charge source copy accompanies distribution. Financial assessment uses a trailing twelve-month basis. Hosted app results differ from a service running customers' own Flows/systems, even if the provider operates execution. Self-service paid enrollment is the predictable way to include coverage for such customers.

The named parent's direct coverage includes later subsidiaries and protects a departing entity's prepaid term. Publisher terms keep their 1% rate and official-release coverage while enrollment continues. These are deliberate reliance protections; they constrain what future relicensing can do to already accepted commercial promises. New commercial agreements may differ, and no release schedule is promised.

## Review method

Independent agents first receive frozen text and isolated assignments. Exact clauses, realistic scenarios, consequences, and the smallest correction are required. The coordinator records accepted, rejected, and unresolved findings and alone integrates edits. Fresh readers later compare the license with its public explanation. Shared filesystem access is not technical isolation; agents are independent model contexts, not lawyers or customers.

Stop after identified material drafting defects are resolved and fresh scenario reviews find no new material contradiction or worthwhile simplification. This is not proof that no improvement exists. Professional legal approval, tax conclusions, market demand, and operational systems cannot be manufactured by model consensus.

## Before adoption

Complete founder/seller identities, actual commercialization authority and customer continuity, publication scope, contribution acceptance, appropriate purchase/consumer/privacy arrangements, and professional review of grants and remedies. Existing private v0.2 founder/operator drafts are background requiring reconciliation, not executed authority.

The owner has explicitly selected a source-available direction despite the current doctrine's OSI commitment. This review does not silently edit that living doctrine or the MPL package notices. Adoption requires synchronized changes to those operative owners. No unrelated technical contract needs changing merely because pricing changes.
