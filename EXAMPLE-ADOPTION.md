are fictional. This example applies the [adoption guide](ADOPTION.md) without
adopting Bread for any real software, offering a purchase, or granting rights.

## Illustrative price schedule E-1

These fictional amounts demonstrate an adopter's commercial choices. They are
neither Bread-wide prices nor prices for any existing product.

| Permission | Illustrative amount and period |
| --- | --- |
| Personal use and genuine evaluation | Free under section 2. |
| Ordinary operations of a qualifying organization | Free while Group Financial Scale is below US$1 million under section 3. |
| Direct organization purchase | US$120 for a twelve-calendar-month Update Term, at any Group Financial Scale. Applicable taxes would be stated separately in the order. |
| Included coverage for a named paid product or service | The standard section 6 agreement: 1% of Covered Receipts, with annual reporting by default or a shorter whole-calendar-month period chosen by the publisher. |

The direct price covers the named Group's ordinary operations, with no seat or
machine limit. The order would identify exact UTC start and end dates, accepted
terms, taxes, and the customer Group. There is no automatic renewal unless
selected. No sponsorship or separate service offer is illustrated.

For example, an Update Term from 2030-01-01 at 00:00 UTC through, but excluding,
2031-01-01 at 00:00 UTC would cover existing official releases and those first
published during that term, subject to exclusions identified before purchase.
Nonrenewal would leave acquired release rights intact; later releases would
need applicable coverage. Growth would not reprice already-acquired rights.

## A paid product with included coverage

Suppose an enrolled publisher delivers a paid inventory application containing
Ledger Engine to a large company. With US$100 in Covered Receipts and no taxes,
refunds, or other deductions, the standard 1% agreement would yield US$1.

The customer could run, modify, and repair the included engine within that
product. Its acquired delivered-copy rights would continue if the publisher
closed. Coverage would not extend to unrelated deployments or the customer's
whole company. Distributed engine source and modifications would accompany
the product; independent application source could remain private.

Recipients of free copies or free customer environments would need their own
applicable permission. People receiving only an application's results would
need no separate engine license; the operator would need coverage. Separately
licensed dependencies would keep their own obligations.

## From illustration to actual adoption

An adopter supplies its own verified identities, authority, release inventory,
contacts, prices, and purchasing and enrollment operations. Contribution
arrangements are its own choice, separate from Bread's fixed bargain. Complete
the [publication prerequisites](ADOPTION.md#before-publishing-a-stable-adoption)
before making an actual offer. This example executes no agreement and changes
no existing license.
