# Bread doctrine — Reciprocity

**Creators provide useful software and dependable freedoms; commercial
participation helps sustain that work and its broad availability.**

This doctrine governs Bread, an independent, reusable licensing model whose
legal instrument is the Bread License. Jig is its first intended adopter.
Bread must serve other maintainers and their users without requiring
participation in Jig or its ecosystem.

## The authority pyramid

```text
                         1. RECIPROCITY
                       Core idea and meaning
                    2. GUIDING PRINCIPLES
                  Commitments that bound decisions
              3. VISION, PURPOSE, MISSION, NORTH STAR
                 The future and outcomes we pursue
             4. DESIGN AND STEWARDSHIP COMMITMENTS
                How the model keeps its promises
           5. LICENSE VERSIONS AND COMMERCIAL CHOICES
              Exact permissions, prices, and processes
                  6. EVIDENCE AND DAILY DECISIONS
             How we test, implement, and improve the model
```

Each lower layer must satisfy every applicable commitment above it. A mission
cannot excuse a broken principle; a revenue target cannot rewrite a customer's
rights. Evidence can motivate a deliberate revision at the owning layer, but
cannot silently change its meaning.

This document governs design and stewardship. Actual permissions and
obligations come from applicable licenses and accepted agreements. A doctrinal
edit cannot amend those grants, replace legal review, or override mandatory law.

## 1. Core idea — Reciprocity

Reciprocity describes a relationship with responsibilities on both sides.
Creators make useful work accessible and give people rights they can depend
on. Commercial participants support that work through clear financial
commitments. Both contribute to conditions in which others can begin, build,
and continue.

The relationship allows unequal contributions. An eligible free user may
never pay, contribute code, promote a project, or become commercially
successful. Their permission is complete within its terms; it creates no
additional debt of money, labor, gratitude, or productivity. Commercial
obligations must be stated, not inferred from a belief that someone has
benefited enough.

Our reason for caring is human: people's lives matter beyond their economic
usefulness. Access can expand what someone can attempt. Income can give a
creator time, security, and the means to continue. Dependable rights can let
another person build a livelihood without requiring continuing discretionary
approval. We value the possibility of living well and meaningfully shaping
one's life, including relationships, rest, learning, and creation.

Reciprocity must constrain the maintainer as well as the paying company.
Receiving payment does not justify surprise restrictions, fragile permissions,
or shifting disproportionate costs onto people further down the chain.

## 2. Guiding principles

### 2.1 Make room to begin

People should be able to learn, experiment, and start building before they
have the resources to fund their tools. Provide meaningful free personal use,
a substantial free organizational allowance, and genuine evaluation. Access
must be useful in practice, with the complete licensed product available.

### 2.2 Treat maintainer livelihoods as a legitimate outcome

Creators should have a straightforward way to receive money for defined
commercial uses. Their own compensation belongs in the purpose of funding.
Support earning from the software itself without requiring a separate
enterprise edition, cloud business, consultancy, or investor.

### 2.3 Make promises dependable

People must be able to plan around the permissions they acquire. Preserve
accepted terms, durable release rights, and continuity through changes in
payment relationships or authorized operators. Commitments must remain
meaningful when changing them would become financially attractive.

### 2.4 Respect the next builder

Downstream developers deserve a workable path to earn from their own products.
Protect independent application ownership, modification, repair, and
predictable distribution. Competition alone is not grounds to withdraw
permission or demand an improvised additional payment.

### 2.5 Make responsible participation straightforward

A company should understand what it receives, what it owes, and how to comply.
Define obligations through published rules and prices. Minimize repeated
classification, reporting, and approval work. Accept some uncollected revenue
rather than burden ordinary users to close every conceivable loophole.

### 2.6 Earn trust through consistent conduct

Use accurate descriptions of rights, restrictions, and evidence. Preserve the
meaning of a published license version. Respect privacy, acknowledge limits,
and distinguish chosen values from demonstrated results. A name or badge must
never substitute for the actual bargain.

## 3. Direction — why we exist and what we pursue

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Vision** | A world in which more people can create useful software, sustain that work, and build independent lives and businesses upon it. |
| **Purpose** | Make broad access and funded development and maintenance compatible with dependable rights for users and downstream builders. |
| **Mission** | Develop, explain, and steward a freely adoptable licensing model that makes those commitments practical for maintainers and understandable to companies. |
| **North star** | A person can begin with few resources, create something useful, earn from it, support the work they depend on, and leave their own customers with dependable rights. |

The north star describes a possibility we want to make easier. It is not a
required journey for every free user. Someone who only learns, experiments,
or enjoys the software also belongs within its free allowance.

For maintainers, success includes income to sustain their work and lives.
For paying companies, it includes useful rights and predictable purchasing.
For users and builders, it includes practical access, control, and continuity.
Tradeoffs are acceptable when their burdens are proportionate and the
commitments above remain intact. Revenue or adoption gains do not compensate
for breaking those commitments.

Recognition matters when it reduces unfamiliar legal reasoning and helps
people understand a real agreement. Adopter counts, publicity, and revenue
alone cannot establish that the model serves its purpose.

Bread's identity expresses tangible usefulness, human livelihoods, and
generosity among equals. Communicate with confidence in the value of the work
and respect for everyone who builds with it. Commercial payments purchase
defined permissions; voluntary support is a separate choice. Avoid appeals to
pity or gratitude, and never make free users feel indebted for their access.

## 4. Design and stewardship commitments

### Access, payment, and continuity

- Keep one complete product under the model. Distinguish permissions and
  optional services without creating a deliberately impaired free edition.
- Make genuine evaluation automatic and purpose-limited, without compulsory
  registration or an arbitrary countdown. Adopted routine work is a separate
  use, even if performed in a development environment.
- Provide published commercial purchasing and distribution terms that let
  people assess their obligations before investing in a product.
- Separate the period for acquiring new-release coverage from the duration
  of acquired release rights. Nonrenewal does not erase those rights. Access
  to an operated hosted service may end with its purchased service period.
- Keep coverage specific to the identified software, organization, product,
  or service. A common license name creates no blanket entitlement across
  unrelated products or licensors.
- Preserve recipients' source access to distributed covered software and its
  modifications. Keep independent application code and outputs under their
  authors' control. Private operation does not itself require publishing
  modifications.

### Proportionate operation

- Keep execution independent of licensing servers, compulsory runtime
  surveillance, activation checks, and remote licensing kill switches.
- Prefer ordinary purchase and accounting records over user tracking or
  detailed deployment inventories. Address evidenced problems proportionately.
- Make free allowances complete within their stated conditions. Evaluation,
  cancellation, and ordinary nonparticipation must not become pressure tactics.
- Design distribution rules for the whole chain. Account for cumulative
  obligations when multiple dependencies use the model. A downstream builder
  must be able to determine the combined obligations and which permissions
  reach its customers before committing to distribution.

### An independently reusable standard

- Make the model's text and adoption materials freely reusable through
  explicit permissions. Adopters should need no membership, royalty to this
  initiative, registration, or discretionary approval merely to use the model.
- Keep core permissions, eligibility definitions, and the distribution
  bargain consistent within each published license version. Identify allowed
  project-specific facts and commercial variables explicitly.
- Require material departures to be identified as modifications. Separate
  permission to adapt the text from permission to claim the standard name.
- Permit clearly identified additional grants that expand rights without
  silently narrowing the standard allowance.
- Let adopters own their software, customer relationships, and commercial
  operations. The initiative does not acquire those assets or become their
  shared seller. Jig, FLOW, and Sley retain their separate ownership and
  licensing decisions.

### Responsible stewardship

Founder stewardship can coexist with public reasoning and independent adoption.
Publish decisions and make settled texts available without requiring a
continuing relationship with the steward. Stewardship does not require a
foundation transfer or give sponsors technical control.

Adopters may choose their own contribution and governance arrangements.
Incoming contributions must supply the authority needed for the declared
licensing strategy, with those requirements disclosed before acceptance.
Control over future licenses does not confer power to revoke acquired grants.
Changes to this doctrine and future versions must be explicit; existing
agreements remain governed by their applicable terms.

## 5. Exact rules and revisable choices

Principles guide the mechanism; they do not mathematically determine a fee,
threshold, assessment period, or price band. Those choices must earn their
burden through the outcomes above.

| Decision | Its proper home and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| Free eligibility, financial definitions, source obligations, and distribution coverage | Exact license text; recognizable and consistent within its version. |
| Distribution royalty and its calculation | The standard commercial bargain for that version; no silent changes for existing agreements. |
| Direct-purchase amounts, permitted price bands, currencies, and update periods | Identified commercial variables and accepted schedules, within the standard rights baseline. |
| Software scope, rights holder, seller, and customer | Adoption notices and purchase records; no implied authority or pooled coverage. |
| Support, sponsorship benefits, and payment operations | Separate offers and processes; no hidden alteration of software rights. |
| Name, website, examples, and outreach | Communication and operation; accurate to applicable terms and principles. |

Changing a draft is different from changing an issued grant. A new version
must identify its differences and respect existing commitments. A doctrinal
revision, new website, or new operator cannot rewrite an accepted bargain.

The present proposal is source-available commercial licensing. Explain its
actual freedoms and restrictions. Do not imply open-source approval, legal
certification, guaranteed maintainer income, or universal procurement acceptance.

## 6. Evidence and the decision test

For a proposed clause, fee, process, or public claim, ask:

1. **Whom does it help?** Identify the creator, free user, paying organization,
   and downstream builder affected, including who bears the burden.
2. **Which principle does it serve?** Explain the connection rather than
   invoking reciprocity as a general permission to collect more money.
3. **What does it preserve?** Check acquired rights, practical access,
   application independence, privacy, and predictable commercial scope.
4. **Can the same purpose be achieved with less burden?** Prefer fewer
   decisions, exceptions, reports, and approval steps where protections remain.
5. **What could show that it is wrong?** Specify an observable failure and
   distinguish a drafting judgment from a market or legal claim.

Use concrete scenarios: someone remains free forever; a sponsor cancels; a
competitor sells cheaply; a publisher closes; a company grows; a maintainer
needs salary; an application contains several licensed dependencies. Each
participant should be able to determine its position from published terms.

Seek evidence from unfamiliar maintainers applying the model, companies
assessing purchases, and developers distributing products. Observe comprehension,
administrative effort, useful access, income, and continuity together. Agent
reviews can expose contradictions; their agreement cannot establish legal
enforceability, audience understanding, or willingness to pay.

When a lower-level choice conflicts with a principle, revise the choice or
explicitly reconsider that principle before proceeding. Neither project
survival nor commercial success is a blanket exception to our commitments.
